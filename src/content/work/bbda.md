title: Seven pieces, seven registers, one school
client: Big Blue Data Academy
kind: Client
year: '2026'
place: Greece
order: 4
problem: A data school needed a feed that never looked like the same advert twice, and still had to be recognisable as one school every time.

brief: |
  Big Blue Data Academy teaches data science to people who are changing careers. The audience is on a phone, in a scroll, and has already learned to skip anything shaped like a course advert.

  The ask was volume. Not one film with a long approval cycle, but a run of short pieces that could go out week after week and keep working.

constraint: |
  Volume and sameness usually arrive together. A house style makes a second piece cheap and a fifth piece invisible, because by then the audience has learned the shape of it and stops looking.

  So the constraint was set the other way round. Every piece had to look like it came from a different production company. The brand had to survive all of them.

built: |
  Seven pieces, each in a register that shares nothing with the others.

  A photoreal punk figure in a dark office, head made of a pie chart, mohawk made of bars. A Pixar-flavoured cartoon detective in a paper-flooded room, magnifier held over a stack of reports. A children's book snake in reading glasses taking notes while a patient lies on a couch. A country singer in a blue Stetson under a saloon sign, playing a song about data.

  Three more went out in registers that are not on this page. Same brand, same fortnight.

  The vertical cuts run at nine by sixteen for the feed. The wide ones run at sixteen by nine.

how: |
  Operator Stack. This is the pipeline that has to be cheap per piece, because the value is in the run and not in any single film.

  The registers were chosen first and written down as a list, before a single frame was made, so that no two briefs could drift into the same look under deadline. Photoreal, 3D cartoon, storybook illustration, live-action pastiche, and three others. A register is a decision about lighting model, edge quality and how much the world is allowed to be wrong.

  Each register got its own plate set and its own prompt vocabulary. Nothing was reused between them, because reuse is exactly the thing that produces the sameness the job was built to avoid.

  The gate here is the brand gate, and it is the only thing that runs across all seven. The mark, the blue and the lockup have to survive a register change without being redrawn. That means the mark is composited, never generated. A model asked to draw a logo will get it nearly right, and nearly right on a logo is worse than absent. The saloon sign is the one place the mark sits inside the world, and that frame was checked letter by letter.

  The second gate is anatomy, which is where the cartoon registers fail first. Hands holding a magnifier, a guitar neck under fingers, a clipboard held by something with no thumbs. Every frame with a held object was checked before it went into a cut.
