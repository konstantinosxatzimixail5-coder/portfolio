problem: A dealership wanted a hero film for a motorcycle it could not get out of the showroom.

brief: |
  Bike Barn sells motorcycles and needed a hero piece for one machine in particular. Something that would stop a thumb on a feed and still read as the actual bike on the actual floor.

  The bike is the product. Everything else in frame is set dressing, and set dressing is the part a dealership never has budget for.

constraint: |
  A motorcycle is the worst possible subject for a generated frame. It carries a badge, a tank graphic and a headlamp cluster that riders know by heart, and it has spokes, which fall apart the moment a model starts inventing detail.

  There was no location, no rain machine, no night permit and no rider. The brief still asked for a wet cobbled street at night with the lights on.

built: |
  A hero piece for the Indian Elite, cut for vertical feed, built around one locked plate of the machine.

  The frame below is the master plate. The tank badge, the fairing line and the headlamp are the three things that had to survive every later render, and they are the three things a rider checks first.

how: |
  Phantom Set again, with the gates turned up.

  The plate came first: the bike alone, correct badge, correct fairing, correct wheel count, nothing else in the picture. The street, the canal houses, the wet cobbles and the reflected lamplight were written as a set specification and reused across every frame, so the lighting could not drift from shot to shot.

  The silhouette gate did the heavy lifting here. Overlay the render on the plate at forty per cent opacity, and any change in the outline of the tank or the fairing kills the frame. Spokes got their own rule: if the count changes between frames, the shot goes back to the plate instead of into a retouching pass.

  The rights gate mattered as much as the visual ones. Two earlier pieces for this client leaned on licensed characters, and those are not on this site for that reason.

landed: |
  Delivered as the hero cut for the model, vertical, with the plate handed over as a reusable file.
